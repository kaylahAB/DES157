Best Practices for Modals and Overlays

October 18, 2026

After reading Naema Baskanderi’s “Best Practices for Modals / Overlays / Dialog Windows,” I realized how easily this pattern can shift from helpful to disruptive. Baskanderi notes that modals began as an elegant way to simplify interfaces and preserve screen space. I initally found them helpful, since they appear with a goal or information, but I argree with her argument that overuse has led to users to dismiss them almost automatically or be assumed as an unpurposful pop-up window. The article’s strongest point is that a modal should only appear when it genuinely serves one of four purposes: capturing attention for something critical, collecting necessary input, showing contextual information, or presenting independent content. Using them for error or success messages, she argues, is noise and a misuse that belongs on the page itself.

What I found most interesting were the emphasis on a clear exit; a cancel button or an escape key these are mentioned to be the practical requirements for a well-built modal, a descriptive title that matches the triggering action that is good sizing so the modal never overwhelms the screen. This made me reflect to be more intentional about asking whether a modal is truly needed or if a simpler, non-modal approach would better respect the user’s flow and not be disruptive during their browsing journey.

Source: Best Practices for Modals / Overlays / Dialog Windows by Naema Baskanderi
