Reflections on UX, design, and practice

October 5, 2026

From reading “Best Practices for Form Design,” I have a better understanding that the most important factors in form and journey design are clarity, cognitive simplicity, and engagement. Users cannot complete a task they don't understand and will most likely abandon tasks that feel too hard. From personal experience, when a page aligns with my expectations and shows clear progress indicators, it feels intuitive; when it doesn’t, disinterest forms and I am more likely to feel confussion or annoyance. What I found interesting in the article is the emphasis on three things: clarity, simplicity, and engagement is what makes or breaks a user expirience. The article made me reflect on and challenge the way I usually think about a page’s interface, as I tend to approach design from a narrative-driven perspective rather than a user-behavior-based one.

When clarity or simplicity is missing, one can easily misinterpret the goals and are more prone to make errors. When filling out unclear forms, I often feel the application is “too long” or “too much work,” which leads me to skim, skip areas, or give low-quality responses. Without these components the form can feel unprofessional and leave applicants indifferent. I believe a core hierarchy (something the reading did not emphasize enough) is what makes the journey more engaging: users must instantly understand what is being asked and why. Without clarity, even a short or “fun” form fails because users hesitate or misinterpret fields. Minimal fields, minimal friction, and minimal decisions reduce scrolling, avoid unnecessary steps, and keep it "clean". Progress indicators, and subtle animations can improve the experience, along with consistent design, colors, button styles, and CSS refinements. These components support trust and usability, but they cannot compensate for unclear or overly complex flows.

Source: Best practices for form design

A form that gets it right

A form format I frequently use is 'Handshake', a job application website for students. It’s easy to navigate and understand, with minimal unnecessary steps. I appreciate forms that ask for little, because it can get tedious and concerning when forms want a plethora of information and require so many steps of verification and confirmation. With Handshake it’s just basic contact and identity verification, then post a resume and cover letter and apply. After the reading and learning more about UX/UI, I think things don’t have to be intricate to be interesting; simplicity is more appreciated than something that has too much noise.
